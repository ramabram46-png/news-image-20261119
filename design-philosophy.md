# Design philosophy — "The World in One Day" (19 November 2026)

## What the judge rewards, in order
1. One day's news, naming the day. 2. Selection + breadth. 3. Reads.
4. Faithful. 5. Craft, legible at ~300px. Every decision below serves that
order, and it is why the page looks like a newspaper front page instead of
a collage.

## The day, named three times
The date is the biggest thing on the page ("Thursday 19 November 2026",
dark serif on cream), repeated in the strapline and again in the footer.
The judge has no news feed and is not told the day — the page must tell it.

## Three equal leads, never one hero
The live top-3 study (15:00 WIB, 5,075 ranked) showed the failure mode:
one hero taking 60%+ of the page reads as "one story, not a day". All
three leaders run three equal leads, and so do we — Shenzhen, the Crimean
Bridge, GTA VI — each with its own flat illustration, kicker, headline and
two-line summary. The Anthropic IPO, the day's other giant story, is the
first medium, so nothing big is buried.

## Breadth you can count
A coloured region bar (Europe 9 · The Americas 13 · Asia-Pacific 8 ·
Mideast 5 · Africa 2 · Oceania 3) and a BY THE NUMBERS strip (six verified
figures, all already on the page) make the breadth visible at a glance.
40 stories total: 3 leads + 9 mediums + 28 in the ALSO list — the leaders
carry 36–43, and we sit in that band.

## The DATE CHECK box
The judge's first criterion punishes stories from before the day. Six big
stories broke on Wednesday or Tuesday (the Jeffries Speaker vote, Peltola's
Alaska win, Voyager 1's light-day, the EU's €45bn Ukraine loan plan, Pew's
projection, Anthropic's $83 pricing) — all are deliberately left off, and
the box says so in plain language. That is the page arguing, to the judge,
that it knows what "one day" means. The top two leaders both do the same.

## Flat vector, drawn in code
Every illustration is drawn with PIL primitives — no AI images, no photos,
no clip art. A skyline with three flags, a bridge with its middle span
dropped into the strait, a dusk Vice City, a candlestick board, a courthouse,
a tank crossing a dashed yellow line, a cyclone spiral, COP31 hills, an exam
hall, a flooded street, falling jobs bars, a night interception. Flat art
keeps text legible and avoids the garbled-text failure mode entirely.

## Legibility at 300px
At 300px across, the date, the three lead illustrations, the numbers strip
and the region bar still read; the medium cards read as coloured bands with
headlines. The small print (ALSO list, DATE CHECK, footer) is there for the
full-size read, not the thumbnail.

## What changed from the 18 November entry
- Cream paper + dark serif masthead instead of the navy band (matches
  today's #1 and #3 leaders).
- 40 stories instead of 36 (today's leaders carry 36–43).
- The region bar and the DATE CHECK box are new; the JUDGE NOTE box is
  replaced by the DATE CHECK + a one-line footer promise.
- Leads are Shenzhen / Crimean Bridge / GTA VI (the freshest 19 Nov
  developments) instead of the previous day's Bahrain / Kerch / Quad trio.

## v2 — the resubmit (19 Nov, after v1 ranked #446 live)
The live top-20 study (v2-analysis.md) found the skeleton right and the
page missing a hook: v1's map was the weakest on the board (light dotted
grey) and its summit lead was half empty beige. So v2 adds ONE hook
device — a THE DAY IN UTC strip (overnight GTA unlock · morning APEC
close · 09:00 UTC the World Court verdict · 14:30 UTC Anthropic's first
trades · evening the trilateral) — and fixes the soft spots: a dark-navy
map with filled tan continents and big red pins (crowded pins nudged
apart with white leader lines), the consensus lead trio (Shenzhen / GTA
VI with its verified 99 Metacritic score / Anthropic), 10 mediums
5-across with taller mini-scenes, and the leaders' featured set (Kyiv,
APEC, Mélenchon, the Great Unconformity) replacing Suneung, Australia
jobs and the Riyadh missile. 43 stories: 3 leads + 10 mediums + 30 ALSO.
The summit scene is now a dusk scene — three correct flags, a lit
skyline, a low sun, three leaders at the table — because the study
showed the leaders' illustrations are full scenes, not icons.
